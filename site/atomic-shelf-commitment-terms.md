# The Atomic Shelf Commitment — Terms

**Status:** Draft, built from the commercial terms defined 2026-09-22. Needs a legal review pass before publishing — this is the plain-language commercial logic, not lawyer-checked language.

---

## 1. What this covers

The Atomic Shelf Commitment applies to every paid plan (Spark, Enhanced, Foundation, Starter, Momentum, Growth) and to Surge/Orbit custom engagements — the same policy applies across all tiers, with no separate terms for custom engagements.

It covers the work Atomic Shelf agrees to deliver under your plan: video production, listing optimization, ARC/review campaigns, preorder campaigns, reader list building, newsletter features, and retargeting, as specified for your plan in the current pricing (or, for Surge/Orbit, as specified in the custom engagement agreement).

## 2. What we promise

We promise to do the work we've committed to, measure what happens, and stay accountable for the outcome. If we fall short of the work, delivery, or agreed commitments on our side, we make it right — at our cost.

## 3. When you're entitled to a refund

You're entitled to a refund of the specific undelivered portion of your plan if, after a full campaign cycle, any of the following is true **despite the agreed work having been carried out as scheduled**:

- Impressions were zero or near-zero
- Click-throughs were zero or near-zero despite measurable impressions
- No measurable results were produced across the full agreed scope of a specific deliverable

In each case, the refund applies only to the line item(s) that were not delivered at all — not a refund of your full plan price, and not triggered by results that are simply lower than the ranges shown on the pricing page.

**This is not a "results were disappointing" refund.** If the work was carried out as agreed and produced some measurable movement — even below the low end of the expected range — that is underperformance, not non-delivery, and is handled under Section 4 instead.

## 4. When results are underperforming (not zero)

If your campaign is producing some measurable results but tracking below the expected range for your plan, we do not treat that as a refund event. Instead, we:

1. Review the account and diagnose what's driving the underperformance
2. Adjust the approach where warranted
3. Continue the agreed work until the cycle completes

Growing a readership takes time. A single month below range is expected variance, not failure — the commitment is to keep working and adjusting, not to guarantee a specific number by a specific date.

## 5. What we do not guarantee

Atomic Shelf does not guarantee:

- A specific sales rank, category ranking, or bestseller position
- A specific number of sales, preorders, or downloads
- An algorithmic viral event
- The behavior of any advertising platform
- Reader behavior generally

## 6. What you need to do

To claim a refund under Section 3, you must **notify Atomic Shelf within 21 days of the end of the campaign cycle** in which the issue occurred (the cycle referenced throughout your plan's reporting, typically the month-3 / 90-day mark). Notify us by emailing nick@atomic-shelf.com with your account details and the specific deliverable(s) you believe were not fulfilled.

Claims raised more than 21 days after the end of the relevant cycle are not eligible for the refund remedy in Section 3, though any underperformance concern is always welcome and will still be reviewed under Section 4.

## 7. How a claim is handled

Once you notify us within the 21-day window, we review the account against the specific trigger conditions in Section 3 — impressions, click-throughs, or delivered scope — and respond with our findings. If the claim is confirmed, the refund is issued for the undelivered portion. If the review finds the work was delivered and simply underperforming, we move to the Section 4 process instead.

## 8. Changes to these terms

These terms apply to the plan and pricing in effect at the time of purchase. Atomic Shelf may update these terms for future customers; existing customers keep the terms that applied when they signed up, for the duration of their current commitment period.
